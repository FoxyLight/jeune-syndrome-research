## Candidate conclusions for approval

- M1. Established as documented clinical practice; comparative benefit unknown. Care combines respiratory stabilization and organ-specific specialist assessment. Oxygen, non-invasive ventilation, invasive ventilation and tracheostomy appear in clinical reports. The appropriate modality and escalation threshold depend on the patient's condition; no uniform Jeune ventilation protocol is established here. [E37-E39, E45, E47-E48]
- M2. Established availability of specialist surgery; preliminary Jeune outcome evidence. VEPTR has United States regulatory clearance for specified thoracic/spinal indications and is offered by a specialist center. Thoracic expansion can be followed by larger chest/lung measurements or reduced support in selected patients, alongside persistent restriction, complications and repeat procedures. Causal survival benefit, best technique and a universal surgery age remain unknown. [E40-E46, E58-E59]
- M3. Probable rationale for continued organ surveillance; exact intervals unknown. Published renal, hepatic and eye schedules differ and have not been prospectively validated in the reviewed evidence. Mild thoracic disease does not remove extra-thoracic risk. Historical advice to reduce follow-up after age 15 is not adopted as an adult-care rule. [E07, E37-E39, E53-E54]
- M4. Preliminary direct evidence of renal replacement and transplant feasibility. Dialysis, kidney transplantation and retransplantation have been reported in selected Jeune cases. These establish documented options and burdens, not disease-wide graft survival, superiority of one approach, or correction of the multisystem disorder. [E37, E50-E51]
- M5. Preliminary hepatic treatment evidence. Ursodeoxycholic acid has reported biochemical responses; prevention of cirrhosis, transplantation or death remains unknown. Liver transplantation is documented for advanced disease, including a recent case with stable graft function and ongoing biliary management. A systemic benefit from genetically intact donor liver remains speculation. [E37, E49, E52-E53]
- M6. Probable need to assess eyes and skeleton; preliminary intervention outcomes. Preserved central visual acuity can coexist with retinal dysfunction. Eye care includes specialist evaluation and functional support when impairment occurs. Selected scoliosis and cervical decompression reports show feasibility, but do not validate universal imaging, prophylactic surgery or comparative safety. [E38, E54-E57]
- M7. Established distinction between practice and proof. Available supportive care and cleared devices are not experimental simply because Jeune-specific evidence is sparse. Novel reconstructions, specialized ventilation claims and biomarkers require separate appraisal. The registry's interventional label can describe diagnostic sampling rather than therapy. Population treatment effects, optimal schedules and genotype-guided management algorithms remain unknown. [E40, E45, E47-E48, E58-E60]

## Respiratory and multidisciplinary care

Ventilatory support assists breathing; oxygen alone addresses oxygenation and is not equivalent to correcting inadequate ventilation. Published cases use both, according to measured respiratory status. Non-invasive ventilation avoids an artificial airway; invasive ventilation uses one. Tracheostomy creates a neck airway. These are documented clinical modalities, not competing Jeune treatment arms. [E37, E45, E47]

Specialist coordination is documented across pulmonary, orthopedic/thoracic, renal, hepatic and eye care. CHOP describes a developing pathway that also includes ENT, neurosurgery and cardiology. Nutrition, swallowing and family communication can matter in reported courses. This records clinical practice rather than a tested team configuration. [E39, E45, E47, E53]

NAVA, support timed to diaphragm electrical activity, was reported in one infant. ECMO, temporary blood oxygenation outside the body, was reported as rescue during influenza respiratory failure in one child. Neither report establishes routine use, a survival advantage or a disease-specific escalation threshold. A mixed thoracic-insufficiency registry is not a Jeune efficacy cohort. [E47-E48]

## Thoracic surgery: benefit signals and burden

Thoracic insufficiency means the chest cannot support normal breathing or lung growth. VEPTR means vertical expandable prosthetic titanium rib. Its device authorization and specialist use are established facts; the benefit of a particular operation for an individual remains a separate clinical decision. [E39-E40]

- O'Brien / Campbell, 2015: 24 treated, two lost; 17 had at least two years of follow-up. Survival of 68% used 22 patients. Mean implant age was 23 months and mean follow-up 8.4 years. Predicted forced vital capacity was 34% at first testing and 27% at last follow-up; assistance change had P=0.07. Migration, infection, wound problems and additional implants were reported. [E08, E41]
- Betz / Campbell, 2008: 19 Jeune and 24 Jarcho-Levin patients. All four deaths were in the Jeune subgroup within one year of initial surgery, with respiratory, renal or liver causes reported. Two of four life-threatening events in the mixed group were perioperative. These do not prove all deaths were caused by surgery. [E42]
- Muthialu, 2014: nine children underwent lateral expansion. No operative deaths, but two deaths within three months from pulmonary hypertension. Some had reduced support; later deterioration and re-expansion also occurred. Severe pulmonary hypertension became an exclusion criterion in that center, not a validated universal rule. [E43]
- Lena, online 2022 / issue 2023: 11 operations in seven ATD patients; five alive and two deaths from sepsis at reported assessment. Median measured lung-volume increase was 88%. Ages, follow-up and detailed complications remain unverified in the abstract. [E44]
- Inserra, 2023: internal sternal distraction in two girls, presenting at six months and two years; reported follow-up six and three years. No further thoracic surgery reported; nocturnal support and respiratory episodes remained. This is preliminary technique evidence. [E45]
- Recent innovations: a 2026 combined Wang/MatrixRIB case reports three-month improvement. A separate mobile chest replacement series contains one Jeune and one Sensenbrenner case. The Jeune child's early improvement was followed by recurrent respiratory problems at about four years and planned further surgery. These are not two independent Jeune successes. [E58-E59]

Chest or CT volume increase is not proof of normalized lung function, fewer deaths or improved quality of life. Surviving operated patients, procedure-level infection rates and patient-level events require different denominators. Some VEPTR cohorts may overlap. No pooled surgical survival or technique ranking is proposed.

The 2024 Chen case documents major postoperative morbidity. Its proposed 6-12-year age range relies on pectus excavatum literature. That transfer does not establish a Jeune timing rule, particularly when other reports describe surgery for severe disease in infancy. Record anatomy, respiratory dependence, lung disease, pulmonary hypertension and multisystem status; leave optimal timing unknown. [E43, E45-E46]

## Renal, hepatic and ophthalmologic surveillance

Two dated proposals illustrate the evidence limit rather than a single recommended schedule. De Vries proposed blood/urine testing twice yearly before age two, then annually; abdominal ultrasound at 2, 5, 10 and 15; annual spirometry from five; and eye assessments at diagnosis, five and ten. Pauli's 2009 expert sheet instead proposes annual kidney/liver laboratory checks, blood pressure at each contact, eye review every three years and electroretinography around six to seven. Neither is a validated universal schedule. [E37-E38]

The proposed kidney assessment includes function, urine and concentration testing; hepatic review includes blood tests and imaging. Organ-specific evaluation and follow-up intensity require clinical interpretation. A normal earlier eye exam or good central acuity does not exclude later or peripheral retinal disease. Optical coherence tomography images retinal layers; electroretinography tests retinal electrical responses. [E07, E37-E38, E54]

The old schedules and their reduction of adult follow-up should not override documented organ heterogeneity. No Jeune-specific renal preventive drug, validated surveillance frequency, or retinal disease-modifying treatment is established by this intake. This is a bounded evidence result, not proof that no such option exists anywhere.

## Renal and hepatic treatment and transplantation

Kidney care is directed to observed dysfunction. The clinical series includes peritoneal dialysis and two transplants in one patient. An independent 1998 report describes a transplant at ten; a 2002 report describes retransplantation after 16 years of first-graft function. These selected cases support feasibility and possible long graft function, with failure/retransplant burdens. They do not estimate current success rates or justify transferring general kidney-disease trial effects to Jeune. [E37, E50-E51]

Ursodeoxycholic acid was reported in three children in 1999 and in the de Vries series. Improvement in liver enzymes is a surrogate outcome; it does not demonstrate prevention of advanced disease. [E37, E49]

The 2003 liver report documents transplantation after portal-hypertension complications. Bakkali's 2026 report describes one 24-year-old man with reported biallelic WDR60 disease, restrictive thorax and advanced liver failure. Following isolated liver transplantation, graft and respiratory function were stable at 36 months, with thrombosis treatment and repeated biliary procedures. The authors explicitly leave a proposed systemic genetic effect untested. No renal-versus-liver or combined-transplant comparison is available here. [E52-E53]

## Orthopedic, cervical and visual function

In two selected patients aged 17 and 24, posterior spinal correction had maintained correction at two and 2.5 years without major reported respiratory complications. One case had previously appeared in a thoracoplasty publication. A 2026 six-patient fusion series adds perioperative observations, but five had previous VEPTR procedures and longitudinal functional outcomes are unverified. Neither establishes safety for all people with Jeune or that fusion improves respiration. [E55, E57]

A 2020 center reported four decompressions among 32 Jeune patients. No postoperative motor/respiratory deficits were reported in those four; follow-up duration and comparative prevention benefit are unverified. Its recommendation for routine screening and prophylactic decompression remains the authors' recommendation. It is not adopted as universal guidance. [E56]

Eye assessment must consider functional symptoms, including night vision and peripheral function, rather than acuity alone. Low-vision services and educational adaptations are documented expert practice when impairment develops. The recent retinal case is diagnostic evidence, not a treatment trial. [E38, E54]

## Verification, uncertainties and stopping state

All management domains in the CP3 contract are addressed. Consequential numerical claims retain source-specific denominators and access limits. Important contradictions include unsupported timing transfer, persistent restriction despite anatomical expansion, postoperative events, and respiratory recurrence despite favorable abstract language. Full-text source and registry details are recorded in the CP3 appendix. Earlier evidence is reused without adding its participants again.

Material limits: small selected samples; inconsistent definitions and follow-up; largely unverified genotypes; possible cohort overlap; abstract-only surgical records; no adequate controlled basis for a causal survival effect; no prospectively validated universal surveillance or management algorithm. Adult-course questions remain for separately authorized CP4.
