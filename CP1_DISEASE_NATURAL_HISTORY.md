## Approved synthesis (submitted candidate text preserved)

### Current understanding

**C1. Definition and classification. Established evidence; candidate project adoption.** Jeune syndrome, also called asphyxiating thoracic dystrophy, is an inherited skeletal disorder within the skeletal ciliopathies. Ciliopathies involve abnormal function of cilia, small cell structures involved in signalling and transport. Inheritance is generally autosomal recessive. Modern classification uses gene-associated short-rib thoracic dysplasia (SRTD), with or without polydactyly. Historical Jeune series and the broader modern SRTD spectrum cannot automatically be treated as identical populations. [E01, E02]

**C2. Clinical spectrum. Established evidence; candidate project adoption.** A narrow thorax with short ribs is central. Characteristic pelvic changes include a three-pronged acetabular roof, the upper part of the hip socket. Limb shortening, short fingers, short stature, and extra fingers or toes vary. Polydactyly is not required, and absence of marked limb shortening does not exclude the phenotype. Presentation ranges from mild disease to severe neonatal respiratory failure. [E03, E06, E09]

**C3. Respiratory course. Probable evidence; candidate project adoption.** Thoracic restriction can limit lung development and breathing. Severe disease can cause neonatal or infant death. Survivors may have recurrent early respiratory infections and continuing support needs. Chest proportions and respiratory symptoms can improve with growth, but improvement is not universal and does not imply normal lung function or resolution of disease. De Vries documented a restrictive spirometry pattern in tested older children and adults despite clinical improvement. [E03, E06, E09]

**C4. Extra-thoracic disease. Established occurrence; unknown population frequencies.** Kidney disease, liver disease, and retinal degeneration are documented, with variable onset and severity. Kidney failure can become a major later complication. Mild thoracic disease does not rule out progressive kidney or retinal disease: the IFT140-associated clinical series illustrates this distinction. Conversely, the DYNC2H1 series had relatively few such findings, with incomplete lifetime observation. The contrast demonstrates heterogeneous study populations, not a deterministic gene-based prediction. Pancreatic abnormalities are reported less often; reliable prevalence and age-specific risks are unavailable here. [E03, E05, E06, E07]

**C5. Age-related changes. Probable respiratory improvement among some survivors; unknown lifetime trajectory.** The literature supports survival into adolescence and adulthood. Skeletal appearance can become less conspicuous while restricted breathing capacity or extra-thoracic disease persists. Adult survivors in small series establish that adulthood is possible, but do not establish average life expectancy, normal adult health, or a predictable age when risk ends. Later-organ complications require a separate outcome from early respiratory survival. Comprehensive adult outcomes remain CP4. [E03, E06, E07]

### Strongest evidence and disagreement

The two 13-case series give materially different outcomes. De Vries reported three deaths among 13 patients, including one unexplained death. Tüysüz included two prenatal cases and reported seven deaths among 11 postnatal patients, six respiratory and one renal. Neither supplies a representative contemporary birth-cohort survival estimate. Differences in referral, diagnostic spectrum, organ involvement, and follow-up are plausible explanations, not demonstrated causes of the discrepancy. Their results are preserved separately. [E03, E04]

Baujat's 53 cases include 30 fetuses and 23 living cases. Its postnatal organ percentages cannot use all 53 as the denominator; its retinal figure concerns only those older than five. O'Brien's reported 68% survival concerns 22 operated patients, while detailed longitudinal analysis concerns 17. It cannot establish population prognosis or a causal surgical benefit against historical mortality figures. No cohorts or review cases are pooled. [E05, E08]

### Survival and treatment-era conclusion

**C6. Contemporary survival probability: unknown.** Frequently repeated 60% to 80% mortality figures must not become a current population estimate or an individual's prognosis. The examined evidence mixes historical series, prenatal ascertainment, genetically selected cohorts, surviving referrals, and surgery-selected patients. No representative contemporary cohort with consistent eligibility, complete follow-up, and a defined survival time point was established in this bounded review. Improved care is a plausible contributor to better outcomes in some reports, but its independent effect is not quantified by these comparisons. [E03-E09]

### Recent developments

The 2025 Pattani study adds a small clinical cohort spanning mild disease and neonatal deaths. Its prenatal associations remain preliminary, without a validated prediction rule. Its accepted manuscript also records incomplete assessment of extra-thoracic features in fatal cases. The 2026 mixed-ciliopathy cohort contains only five SRTD cases, and the 2026 IFT74 report is one infant. Neither resolves contemporary survival or broad organ frequencies. [E09-E11]

The registered prospective natural-history study NCT00948376 is marked completed and lists actual enrollment of 50, but no results are posted in the current registry response. Its planned narrative of 50 patients and 20 fetuses is not an observed outcome denominator. Its relationship to published cohorts was not verified. [E12]

### Limitations and uncertainty

- This is a bounded evidence review, not a systematic review or meta-analysis. The recent title screen does not guarantee that every relevant publication was retrieved.
- Small samples, variable diagnostic and genetic composition, unequal follow-up, prenatal selection, survivor selection, treatment selection, and possible repeated families limit quantitative comparisons.
- Some influential sources were available only as abstracts. Their unverified details do not support stronger claims. Rates are retained as source-specific observations, not adopted as disease-wide estimates.
- Contemporary age-specific survival, incidence, organ complication rates, and independent effects of treatment era remain unresolved.
- CP2 genetics, CP3 management and surveillance schedules, CP4 adult outcomes, and CP5 frontier work have not started. No individualized clinical recommendation is proposed.
