## Evidence and interpretation rules

Create an evidence register when authorized intake begins. Capture the master policy's available citation, identifier, publication date, study design, participant count, age, diagnostic definition, genotype/variant, phenotype, intervention, comparison, outcome, follow-up, findings, limitations, relevance, confidence, checkpoint, and authority fields. Mark missing details as not reported or not verified; do not invent them.

Every substantive medical claim must be traceable to evidence. Separate observation from hypothesis, direct evidence from inference, and absence of reporting from evidence of absence. Identify duplicate patients, families, overlapping cohorts, follow-up reports, and review restatements. Preserve suspected overlap until resolved.

Account for rare-disease heterogeneity, small samples, treatment era, diagnostic definitions, ascertainment, survival and publication bias, referral patterns, follow-up, and access to care. Compare conflicting evidence without manufacturing consensus. Do not infer deterministic prognosis or variant pathogenicity from a reported association.

Authority states are **exploratory**, **candidate**, **authoritative**, and **historical**. A verified candidate remains candidate until explicitly approved and integrated. Evidence confidence is a separate dimension: **established**, **probable**, **preliminary**, or **unknown**, using the master policy definitions. Neither dimension predicts an individual's outcome.
