## Definitions and decision standard

Cilia are small cellular structures that coordinate transport and signaling. An organoid is laboratory-grown tissue that models some organ features. An isogenic control has the same genetic background except for the intended change. Rescue means a measured defect improves after an intervention; it does not necessarily mean cure. An antisense oligonucleotide is a short synthetic molecule that can change RNA processing. A hypomorphic allele retains some gene function.

For this assessment, a whole-disease cure would require durable correction of relevant disease processes with meaningful function and acceptable safety. Preventing skeletal abnormalities before birth, slowing postnatal kidney or retinal disease, and reversing existing thoracic abnormalities are separate goals. An organ-specific treatment could be valuable without meeting the whole-disease cure definition. This is a research decision standard, not a validated clinical endpoint.

## Candidate conclusions for approval

- F1. Unknown curative efficacy. No demonstrated whole-disease cure or Jeune-directed disease-modifying treatment trial was identified in this bounded search. The registry result is an observed search finding, not proof that no unregistered or unpublished program exists. Approved supportive care, surgery and transplant evidence retains its CP3 limits. [M1-M7, E74]
- F2. Preliminary evidence that restoring a relevant gene can rescue laboratory defects. IFT140 correction improved kidney-organoid abnormalities in one Mainzer-Saldino proband; KIAA0586 expression rescued patient-cell ciliogenesis in a Jeune-Joubert study; DYNC2LI1 complementation illustrates allele-combination dependence. None established delivery, skeletal benefit or cure in people. [E61-E63]
- F3. Preliminary mechanistic opportunities, unknown universal target. IFT80/GLI2 experiments and Dync2h1 genetic suppression support testing downstream or transport-balance approaches. Failure of Hedgehog stimulation in other models and altered signaling despite normal ciliation prevent a universal "increase cilia" or "activate Hedgehog" rule. [E32, E64-E67]
- F4. Preliminary shared drug leads, unknown Jeune benefit. Renal fish experiments, USP35/USP38 suppression and newer prostaglandin/eupatilin work justify model-specific investigation. Different compounds improved different endpoints; improved ciliation did not consistently improve kidney cysts. No treatment recommendation follows. [E68, E71-E72]
- F5. Preliminary RNA/editing transferability. CEP290 kidney rescue and human retinal editing provide relevant platform precedents in different disorders. They do not establish a Jeune treatment. Exon skipping requires gene/variant compatibility; the CEP120 assessment specifically argues against applying its approach indiscriminately. [E69-E70, E73]
- F6. Unknown whole-body delivery and reversal after development. This intake provides no proof that genetic rescue can restore an established narrow thorax, correct multiple organs, or work safely after disease develops. Laboratory correction before tissue differentiation and embryo rescue cannot answer that question. Delayed-treatment and organ-function evidence are essential before translational claims. [E61-E73]
- F7. Recommendation, not an established therapy. Prioritize access to a human skeletal-ciliopathy model with disease-relevant alleles and a corrected control, then test whether genetic restoration improves cartilage differentiation as well as ciliary transport/signaling. Start by verifying model access and feasibility with a relevant laboratory. The ranking reflects relevance to the chest-focused disease question, not a demonstrated probability of cure. [E61, E64-E67, E75]

## Gene and approach comparison

- Dynein-2 group: DYNC2H1, DYNC2LI1, DYNC2I1/WDR60, DYNC2I2/WDR34 and DYNLT2B/TCTEX1D2. Consider allele correction or functional gene restoration first as a mechanistic benchmark. DYNC2LI1 rescue is combination-dependent; newer WDR models reproduce human alleles but were not treated. DYNC2H1 transport-balance suppression is an embryonic mouse result. Delivery, correct expression and cartilage benefit are unresolved. [G1-G6, E63-E64, E67]
- IFT-A group: IFT140, WDR19, TTC21B, IFT122, WDR35 and IFT43. IFT140 renal organoids provide a useful corrected-control precedent. It does not establish class-wide rescue or chest reversal. For the other genes, no human disease-modifying rescue is adopted here; this is a coverage limit, not absence of biological opportunity. [GENETICS_MAP, E61]
- IFT-B group: IFT80, IFT172, IFT81, IFT52, TRAF3IP1/IFT54 and additional IFT74 evidence. IFT80 has relevant cartilage-cell rescue; TRAF3IP1 fish contribute renal drug evidence. Gene restoration and pathway approaches require separate testing for each allele and tissue. [GENETICS_MAP, E65-E68, E72]
- Assembly, signaling and additional associations: CEP120, CFAP410, KIAA0586, KIAA0753 and GRK2; FGF4 remains a preliminary distinct association. KIAA0586 patient-cell rescue is in a hybrid phenotype. GRK2 evidence shows that normal cilia appearance can coexist with signaling defects. The approved map's related NEK1/INTU, CILK1 and CSPP1 classifications remain separate. No universal platform suitability or new association is inferred. [G1-G6, E32, E35, E62, E70]

This comparison uses the approved genetics map as the gene universe. It is not a claim that every gene has an equally mature treatment program or that all related diagnoses are classic Jeune.

## Ranked research paths and decisive gaps

1. Human skeletal gene-rescue model. Highest relevance to testing the disease cause. Existing WDR34/WDR60 models and patient-cell experience offer a starting point. Missing: restoration in human disease-relevant cartilage, reproducibility across alleles, and rescue after differentiation. A kidney model alone cannot answer the thoracic question. [E61-E67]
2. Organ-specific renal rescue. Stronger existing human-tissue model precedent through IFT140, but narrower relevance to whole-disease cure. Missing: durable organ function, appropriate renal delivery and evidence beyond one corrected proband line. [E61]
3. Shared small-molecule or suppressor approaches. Potentially broader coverage, but early and inconsistent across endpoints. Missing: reproducible rescue in Jeune-relevant human alleles and cartilage, then meaningful organ function and safety. [E68, E71-E72]
4. Variant-specific RNA or in vivo editing. Relevant precedents exist outside Jeune, with substantial gene/variant, tissue-delivery and timing constraints. Missing: an eligible Jeune variant, a validated functional product, relevant tissue delivery and sustained benefit. These are not ready-to-use Jeune treatments. [E69-E70, E73]

No chance-of-success estimate, cure timeline, budget or clinical efficacy ranking is supported. Existing clinical surgery belongs to management and does not establish molecular correction. [M1-M7]

## Proposed next experiment, for review only

Research question: Does restoring a defective dynein-2 gene in a human disease-allele model improve both ciliary function and cartilage development, and does benefit persist when restoration begins after differentiation?

First action: ask a qualified laboratory whether suitable existing human material, genetic controls and cartilage assays are available before selecting a gene or funding new model construction. The Schmidts group is the first capability lead, not a confirmed partner.

Conceptual comparison: disease model, genetically matched corrected model, unaffected reference, and an appropriate sham/intervention control. Genetic rescue is the benchmark before judging a shared drug. Require an upstream endpoint appropriate to the allele, such as transport or signaling, and a cartilage endpoint, such as matrix formation or maturation, with toxicity and abnormal-growth assessment. Specify quantitative endpoints, independent replication and analysis before experimentation with the responsible scientists. This report does not invent a powered sample size or an approved experimental protocol.

Decision: pursue a treatment lead only if relevant functional rescue is reproducible and not explained by toxicity or nonspecific growth suppression. A result limited to cilia length is insufficient. If correction fails in the disease-relevant tissue, reconsider the model/mechanism before drug screening. If correction works only before differentiation, retain prevention and postnatal treatment as separate hypotheses. An animal thoracic/respiratory and delivery study would be a later, separately justified step.
