## Search, eligibility, and access

Foundational and citation-chain searches used Jeune syndrome, Jeune asphyxiating thoracic dystrophy, asphyxiating thoracic dystrophy, natural history, survival, clinical cohorts, short-rib thoracic dysplasia, and skeletal ciliopathies. PubMed, publisher sites, PMC/Europe PMC, an academic manuscript repository, NIH information, and ClinicalTrials.gov were consulted. Search date is 2026-10-02, not a publication date.

The bounded recent Europe PMC query was `(TITLE_ABS:"Jeune syndrome" OR TITLE_ABS:"Jeune asphyxiating" OR TITLE_ABS:"asphyxiating thoracic" OR TITLE_ABS:"short-rib thoracic dysplasia" OR TITLE_ABS:"skeletal ciliopathies") AND FIRST_PDATE:[2023-01-01 TO 2026-10-02]`, core records, page size 100, date ordering. All 68 returned titles were screened; relevant recent cohorts and a severe contemporary case were examined below. This is not a systematic search or a count of eligible studies. The initial broader Jeune query produced unrelated French-language results and was not treated as an eligible evidence set.

Excluded from inherited-disease natural history: acquired secondary thoracic dysplasia due to rib chondromas, PMID 40824053. Surgical spine cohorts remain CP3 context, with possible overlap with earlier treated cohorts. Prenatal-only reports do not provide live-born survival denominators. Qualitative family accounts are distinct evidence, not outcome rates. Preclinical biomarker observations do not imply clinical treatment.

Primary PMC XML was inspected for E03 and E06; publisher full text for E02 and E07; accepted author manuscript for E09. E04, E05, E08, and E11 remain abstract-level. E10 was the publisher's early accepted report and abstract, not a verified full clinical record. Public metadata/abstract and registry captures were kept outside repository artifacts in the task's source cache. Retrieval failures and browser challenges were not counted as successful full-text access.

## E01: Authoritative disease description

National Library of Medicine. *Asphyxiating thoracic dystrophy*. MedlinePlus Genetics. [NIH page](https://medlineplus.gov/genetics/condition/asphyxiating-thoracic-dystrophy/). Page revision date not displayed in accessed text; accessed 2026-10-02. Educational reference, not a primary clinical study; sample, age, follow-up, intervention, and comparator not applicable. Broad Jeune/ATD phenotype and autosomal recessive inheritance. Supports terminology and general spectrum, not a quantified prognosis. Older gene counts and incidence estimates were not adopted. Confidence: established for basic description; quantitative currency unverified.

## E02: Classification authority

Unger S, Ferreira CR, et al. *Nosology of genetic skeletal disorders: 2023 revision*. Am J Med Genet A. 2023;191A:1164-1209. Online 2023-02-13. DOI [10.1002/ajmg.a.63132](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ajmg.a.63132); PMID 36779427; PMC10081954. Expert classification, not a cohort. Group 10 places gene-associated SRTD within disorders of cilia or ciliary signalling and retains former ATD/Jeune labels. Supports nomenclature; no survival or complication frequencies. No newer revision was established in this bounded search. Confidence: established classification reference, not a diagnostic or prognostic rule.

## E03: Foundational clinical natural-history series

de Vries J, Yntema JL, van Die CE, Crama N, Cornelissen EAM, Hamel BC. *Jeune syndrome: description of 13 cases and a proposal for follow-up protocol*. Eur J Pediatr. 2010;169:77-88. Online 2009-05-10. DOI [10.1007/s00431-009-0991-3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00431-009-0991-3); PMID 19430947; PMC2776156. Full text, Methods, Table 1, spirometry and Discussion checked.

Retrospective Nijmegen clinical/radiographic ATD series: 13 patients, 11 families. Reported examination ages nine months to 22 years exclude earlier deceased cases. Eleven living patients examined; one died subsequently. Three deaths: respiratory at five weeks and two years; unexplained at 18 months. Ten survivors at study end. Respiratory burden often decreased with age, while tested patients older than five had restrictive spirometry. Progressive renal failure occurred in one clinical case, plus renal pathology at neonatal autopsy; liver findings also occurred. Care varied, including respiratory support, transplantation, and one thoracic reconstruction. No controlled comparator or uniform survival follow-up. Historical mortality statements are cited background, not this series' outcome. Referral, age, and possible overlap limit rates. Confidence: probable qualitative course; unknown population prognosis.

## E04: Materially different outcomes

Tüysüz B, Barış S, Aksoy F, Madazlı R, Üngür S, Sever L. *Clinical variability of asphyxiating thoracic dystrophy (Jeune) syndrome: Evaluation and classification of 13 patients*. Am J Med Genet A. 2009;149A:1727-1733. Publisher online 2009-07-16; August issue. DOI [10.1002/ajmg.a.32962](https://doi.org/10.1002/ajmg.a.32962); [PMID 19610081](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19610081/).

Primary abstract checked; full text unavailable. Clinical series: 13 patients, 11 families, including two prenatally diagnosed autopsy cases. Among 11 postnatal patients, eight had respiratory distress, six died from it, one died from chronic renal failure, and four remained alive. Age range, observation intervals, treatments, diagnostic detail, and genotype composition not verified. Proposed phenotype/prognosis groups are exploratory, not validated predictors. Its postnatal denominator differs from E03's total denominator. No pooling. Confidence: probable reported outcomes; unknown generalizability.

## E05: Mixed prenatal and postnatal clinical series

Baujat G, Huber C, El Hokayem J, et al. *Asphyxiating thoracic dysplasia: clinical and molecular review of 39 families*. J Med Genet. 2013;50:91-98, February issue. Exact online date not verified. DOI [10.1136/jmedgenet-2012-101282](https://doi.org/10.1136/jmedgenet-2012-101282); [PMID 23339108](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23339108/).

Primary abstract checked; full text unavailable. Deep phenotyping and IFT80/DYNC2H1 screening of 53 ATD cases from 39 families: 23 living and 30 fetuses. Among 23 postnatal cases, reported pulmonary insufficiency 60%, five tracheotomies, renal disease 17%, liver disease 22%. Retinal alterations 50% refers only to those older than five; subgroup count and longitudinal ages not verified. Incomplete molecular findings and selected living/fetal composition preclude a birth-cohort prognosis. Relationship to E12 not verified; possible overlap with other molecular series. Confidence: preliminary source-specific rates; established occurrence of organ involvement with corroboration.

## E06: DYNC2H1-associated clinical variability

Schmidts M, Arts HH, Bongers EM, et al. *Exome sequencing identifies DYNC2H1 mutations as a common cause of asphyxiating thoracic dystrophy (Jeune syndrome) without major polydactyly, renal or retinal involvement*. J Med Genet. 2013;50:309-323. Online 2013-03-01. DOI [10.1136/jmedgenet-2012-101284](https://doi.org/10.1136/jmedgenet-2012-101284); PMID 23456818; [PMC3627132](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3627132/).

Original full text checked, clinical results, tables and discussion. Molecular study of 71 JATD patients from 57 families; DYNC2H1 findings in 29 individuals from 19 families. Clinical ages include fetuses, children, adolescents and adults; variable follow-up, not a uniform longitudinal cohort. Thoracic severity differed even between siblings, with improvement in some older survivors. Renal/retinal abnormalities were reported in two affected individuals; liver findings also occurred. Unassessed lifetime risk and terminated fetuses limit absence claims. Shared recruitment and published families raise possible overlap with E03/E05/E07; no pooling. No treatment comparison. Confidence: probable clinical heterogeneity; limited subtype-specific organ-risk inference.

## E07: Progressive renal and retinal disease despite mild thorax

Schmidts M, Frank V, Eisenberger T, et al. *Combined NGS approaches identify mutations in the intraflagellar transport gene IFT140 in skeletal ciliopathies with early progressive kidney disease*. Hum Mutat. 2013;34:714-724, May issue. Exact online date not verified. DOI [10.1002/humu.22294](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/humu.22294); PMID 23418020; PMC4226634. Publisher full clinical text and discussion checked.

Molecularly selected JATD/Mainzer-Saldino syndrome study. Text reports 64 JATD plus two MSS screened, four JATD and two MSS families with biallelic findings, and an additional JATD family with only one detected allele. Abstract describes five JATD and two MSS families; discussion also varies patient/family denominator wording. Do not silently equate these denominators. Clinical cases had early progressive renal disease and generally mild thoracic disease; retinal findings could emerge after normal earlier examinations. Ages and follow-up vary; some lost follow-up. The authors' classifications are reported, not independently reassessed. Renal referral and molecular selection, small numbers, mixed diagnoses, and potential overlap prevent prevalence estimates. Confidence: probable qualitative discordance between thoracic and organ severity.

## E08: Selected surgical cohort, contextual only

O'Brien A, Roth MK, Athreya H, et al. *Management of Thoracic Insufficiency Syndrome in Patients With Jeune Syndrome Using the 70 mm Radius Vertical Expandable Prosthetic Titanium Rib*. J Pediatr Orthop. 2015;35:783-797, December issue. Exact online date not verified. DOI [10.1097/BPO.0000000000000383](https://doi.org/10.1097/BPO.0000000000000383); [PMID 25575358](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25575358/).

Primary abstract checked. Twenty-four operated patients, two lost; 17 had minimum two-year longitudinal follow-up. Mean implant age 23 months, range 7-62; mean follow-up 8.4 years, range 2.3-15.6. Reported survival 68% uses 22 patients, not 17. No concurrent untreated comparator. Historical mortality comparison does not identify a causal treatment effect. Average forced vital capacity remained low; ventilatory-assistance change had P=0.07 and is not treated as demonstrated improvement. Diagnostic/genotype and detailed outcome timing not verified. Later surgical reports may overlap. Confidence: preliminary contextual evidence of persistent burden; population and treatment-effect estimates unknown. Management recommendations deferred to CP3.

## E09: Recent heterogeneity and limited prenatal prediction

Pattani N, Elkhateeb N, Joshi A, et al. *Phenotypic heterogeneity in DYNC2H1-related short-rib thoracic dysplasia: antenatal indicators and postnatal outcomes*. J Med Genet. 2025;62:476-483. Indexed publication 2025-06-24; exact initial online date not verified. DOI [10.1136/jmg-2024-110369](https://doi.org/10.1136/jmg-2024-110369); PMID 40250984; [accepted author manuscript](https://api.repository.cam.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/72d0b133-9eba-44cf-b588-c461ae55ffaa/content). Full manuscript, Tables 1-2 and Discussion checked; copyedited version not compared.

Retrospective series: nine individuals, eight families, three English tertiary centres. Four mild, two moderate, three neonatal deaths on days 2, 3 and 9. Survivors aged 28 days to 11 years; follow-up unequal. Five of six had early respiratory admissions. Prenatal associations lack external validation. Fatal cases lacked extra-thoracic assessment; an included variant remained uncertain. No controlled treatment comparison; none had thoracic expansion. Confidence: probable heterogeneity; preliminary prediction. Historical mortality is cited through E03, not newly measured. Manuscript text/reference alignment has inconsistencies; cited older results were checked against their original sources.

## E10: Contemporary severe case

Abadi A, Ateeq B, Kittaneh H, Ghanem U, Abu Hajji R. *Asphyxiating thoracic dystrophy associated with IFT74 pathogenic variant: a case report*. BMC Pediatr. Published 2026-08-04; accepted 2026-07-31. DOI [10.1186/s12887-026-07472-4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12887-026-07472-4). Publisher early accepted abstract checked, not complete clinical text.

One term infant with narrow chest, hypotonia, dysphagia/aspiration, reported IFT74 copy loss, and death at four months. Skeletal imaging described as inconclusive. Variant pathogenicity is the authors' claim, not independently verified here. No comparator, representative denominator, or disease-wide neurologic inference. Confidence: preliminary case evidence that severe outcomes remain reported in contemporary care, not an estimated risk.

Biomarker results do not establish natural history or therapy. Confidence: preliminary contextual evidence; no quantitative CP1 conclusion adopted.

## Evidence decision

Qualitative definition, heterogeneity, possible early respiratory lethality, survival into adulthood, and variable later organ involvement are sufficiently corroborated for the CP1 candidate. The direction of respiratory improvement among some survivors is probable. Contemporary population survival, lifetime organ risks and prenatal prediction performance remain unknown or preliminary. No pooled estimate, gene-specific prognosis, surveillance schedule, treatment efficacy claim, or clinical variant reassessment is proposed.
